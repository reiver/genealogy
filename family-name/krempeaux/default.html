Krempeaux (Family Name)

Krempeaux ← Krempo ← Krempowiecki ← Krępowiecki

by Charles Iliya Krempeaux

Krempeaux is a French spelling of the shortened version of an English spelling of a family-name with a Polish origin.

‘Krempeaux’ is my family-name!

Krempeaux is pronounced krem-po. The “eaux” part at the end Krempeaux makes an “o” sound.

You can understand the etymological evolution of the Krempeaux family-name with the following figure:

The original spelling of ‘Krempeaux’ is “Krępowiecki” — pronounced krem-po-vyet-ski.

Note that the third (3rd) letter of ‘Krępowiecki’ is the Polish letter “ę” and not the English letter “e”. I.e., the third (3rd) letter of ‘Krępowiecki’ is the Polish letter e with a little tail — e z ogonkiem — and not an “e”. The Polish letter “ę” (e with a little tail (e z ogonkiem)) makes an “em” sound.

‘Krępowiecki’ became “Krempowiecki”. ‘Krempowiecki’ became “Krempo”. And, ‘Krempo’ became “Krempeaux”. (The details of these etymological transformations are covered later in this article.)

I have seen a Russian Cyrillic spelling of “Krępowiecki”: Кремповецкий. Although it (Krępowiecki) is not a Russian name, because Tadeusz Szymon Krępowiecki is considered historically noteworthy to Russians, there is a Russian Cyrillic spelling, too.

Origins of the Family-Name

I think I figured out the origin of the ‘Krempeaux’ family-name. It took me several decades to discover it. (And, much of that time was pre- mass-Internet.) But, I think I have a number of the important details.

When I was very young, I remember my aunt Audrey verbally telling me the original version of the family name. (I didn't see it written down.) I remember what she told me sounding something like "Kremposki". It wasn't "Kremposki" — but that is what I remembered.

On Friday January 15th, 2016 I found a document that suggested the original spelling of the family name was: "Krempowiecki". It wasn't the "original" spelling — but it was close. See: "The London Gazette, 10th February 1959":

My paternal grandfather Charles Stanislaus Krempeaux's name appears in the document (although with his middle name spelled "Stanislas"). He appears in the same record as Charlotte Elizabeth Krempowiecki. In particular, it looks like Charles Stanislaus Krempeaux was dealing with Charlotte Elizabeth Krempowiecki's death. I already knew that Charles Stanislaus Krempeaux had two (2) sisters: Charlotte and Violet.

A little investigation suggested that "Krempowiecki" is an anglicized spelling of the Polish name: "Krępowiecki". (Where the Polish letter "ę" is an "em" sound, and gets turned into "em" when written in English.)

I asked a native Polish speaker (the mother of a close friend) how "Krępowiecki" is pronounced. I was told: krem-po-vyet-ski.

In addition to "Krempeaux", I have also seen some records from family members in England who spelt it as: "Krempo".

This seems to be consistent with what I have heard about French Family Names That End With an X — that it usually signifies someone who was illiterate in French or of foreign (non-French) origin.

As far as I can tell, there was three (3) transformations that my family-name went through to end up at ‘Krempeaux’:

We will look at each of these.

Here is the etymological origins of the ‘Krempeaux’ family-name. How ‘Krępowiecki’ became “Krempowiecki”, and ‘Krempowiecki’ became “Krempo”, and ‘Krempo’ became “Krempeaux”.

The First Krępowiecki

The original spelling of the ‘Krempeaux’ family-name was ‘Krępowiecki’ — pronounced krem-po-vyet-ski.

The origin of the ‘Krępowiecki’ family-name, and thus the ‘Krempeaux’ family-name, goes back to Adam Krępowiecki. (Ancestors of Adam Krępowiecki would have used patronyms rather than a family-name.)

Adam Krępowiecki took on the Krępowiecki family-name when he was baptized with a group of Frankists in the year 1759 in the city of Lwów (i.e., Lviv).

Adam Krępowiecki may have had a different name before he was baptized.

Krępowiecki → Krempowiecki

This Krępowiecki to Krempowiecki transformation was due to Tadeusz Szymon Krępowiecki.

(Tadeusz Szymon Krępowiecki seems to be considered historically important in certain parts of the world, so one can find information written about him. For example, school children in Poland are taught about Tadeusz Szymon Krępowiecki when learning about Polish history. In addition to that, one can find his own writings.)

(Tadeusz Szymon Krępowiecki is my father's father's father's father.)

When Tadeusz Szymon Krępowiecki left Poland and eventually ended up in England, his family-name ‘Krępowiecki’ was anglicized (given an English spelling) as: “Krempowiecki”. Note that “ę” (e with a little tail (e z ogonkiem)) in ‘Krępowiecki’ became an “em” in ‘Krempowiecki’.

Krempowiecki → Krempo

The family-name ‘Krempowiecki’ was shortened to ‘Krempo’. I don't know when this first started, but I have seen records of Tadeusz Szymon Krępowiecki grandchildren. Some of these records show their family-name as ‘Krempowiecki’. While, other records show their family-name as ‘Krempo’. The grandchildren being:

  • Charlotte Elizabeth Krempowiecki / Charlotte Elizabeth Krempo
  • Violet Krempowiecki / Violet Krempo
  • Charles Stanislaus Krempowiecki / Charles Stanislaus Krempo / Charles Stanislaus Krempeaux

This practice (of shortening the ‘Krempowiecki’ family-name to ‘Krempo’) may have started earlier, but so far this has been the earliest I have seen the Krempo family-name being used.

(One of Tadeusz Szymon Krępowiecki's grandchildren (Charles Stanislaus Krempeaux) is my father's father.)

Krempo → Krempeaux

Someone with the family-name ‘Krempo’ changed it to ‘Krempeaux’. Basically, the family-name ‘Krempo’ was given a French spelling — changing it to ‘Krempeaux’. From what I can tell that someone was my paternal grandfather, Charles Stanislaus Krempeaux. This may have been due to him being in France.

Charles Stanislaus Krempeaux was born in London, English (despite some records saying he was born in Paris, France). But, I have heard multiple family members saying Charles Stanislaus Krempeaux lived in Paris, France. I even heard that Charles Stanislaus Krempeaux lived in Paris while the Eiffel Tower was being built. And, I heard that both his parents died in a train crash when he was young. I wonder if that event (his parents dying) somehow relates to him growing up in Paris, France.

Later, Charles Stanislaus Krempeaux came to Canada, married Helen Elizabeth Kerr, and had 3 children: Paul Douglas Krempeaux, Charles Robert Krempeaux, and Audrey Jane Krempeaux. And of course, grandchildren, and great grandchildren, etc came after that.

(Charles Robert Krempeaux is my father.)

Note that I have seen 1 record for Charles Stanislaus Krempeaux where the “Name of Next of Kin” is written as “Charlotte Krempeaux”. This may have been written by someone other than Charles Stanislaus Krempeaux who assumed that Charles Stanislaus Krempeaux's sister Charlotte had the same family-name as him. Polish Family Names That End With "ski"

My understanding is that Polish names that end with "ski" (or "ki" in the case of "Krępowiecki", although it is pronounced "ski") means "from some city" or "of some city". In the case of "Krępowiecki" it means "from Krępowiec" or "of Krępowiec"; where "Krępowiec" may have been a city, town or village.

In Poland, these type of family names were members of the szlachta -- members of the Polish nobility. At that time in history, about 10% to 30% of the Polish population was szlachta.

What a name like "Krępowiecki" effectively meant is that at one time, there may have city, town, or village named ""Krępowiec" and the "Krępowiecki" family owned it.

I have not yet been able to find any town in Poland named "Krępowiec". So (if I got the spelling of the suspected city, town, or village correct) it may have either been renamed or no longer exists.
